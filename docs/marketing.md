# Marketing

Faceless. The ads show the kit, the scripts, or the ad swipe. They do not show you. They do not use a fake agent. They do not promise income.

## One destination

Every front end uses the same path:

1. Sales page for that offer.
2. The same checkout.
3. A $17 order bump: editable Canva versions of the piece they just bought.
4. One-time offer: founder membership at $97/mo while seats remain, then $147/mo. Setup is waived because they bought a front end.
5. Thank-you page with the download and the community invite.

The physical kit is not on this path. thanks.io ships only to the customers of members, after a first payment, as a referral and word-of-mouth tool for that member’s business.

Digital delivery has to land in under two minutes. That is P01.

## Tests

- $300 to $500 per front end.
- $2,000 total in each of November, December, and January.
- Kill it if a sale costs more than $100.
- Kill it if fewer than 10% of buyers take membership.
- Do not spend until the domain is registered and you have recorded the trademark search.

Our buyers are agents. The ads still follow the special-ad-category rules: no age call-out, no ZIP-only targeting, no lookalike. HighLevel Ad Manager can set the Financial Products category. Use it when the ad is about insurance business.

## Angles

Use these as themes. The sentence on the ad has to describe the piece being sold. It cannot promise the commission returns.

- Months 2–6 are when policies fall off, and the kit is the follow-up.
- Clients forget the agent. The fridge sheet keeps the name in the kitchen.
- Meta limits age targeting. The creative has to do the work.
- Final-expense ads get rejected. These concepts are written for that review.
- A client knows other people who need the same help. The referral script asks.

## Creative rules

- Product on screen. No founder face.
- No client or agent testimonial, staged or real, in v1 ads.
- No carrier name, no premium, no “low cost,” no Medicare.
- No income claim and no guaranteed result.
- You approve the set before it runs.

## Over-deliver

The front end is cheap relative to what is in it: a letter, a magnet design, a family sheet, and the scripts a member will later run on every customer. The membership is the system that sends those pieces for them. The kit their customer receives is billed at twice mail cost, not marked up like a gift boutique.

Do not add a call to make the offer feel bigger. If ad-setup tickets ever exceed 30% of active members in a month, one weekend group call can be added. Until then, support stays in the community, the videos, and the AI agent.
